Description

How can we trust an AI agent to reconstruct, generate, or edit a 3D world?

LLM and multimodal agents increasingly build and revise 3D/4D models through tools such as Blender. A convincing render, however, can hide incorrect geometry, unintended edits, or invented scene details. Malicious instructions in scene content or tool responses can also redirect an agent's actions, turning a modeling workflow into a security risk.

TrustAgent3D brings together vision, graphics, foundation-model, and security researchers to address these failures. We focus on semantic attacks, hallucination, and reliable tool use, supported by independent checks and formal verification. Our goal is to establish what evidence an agent needs before trusting a scene claim, executing an edit, or reporting success, while preserving useful modeling capabilities and enabling recovery.

Topics

  • Semantic attacks and defenses. Prompt injection, jailbreaks, and malicious scene or tool content; defenses that preserve legitimate task completion.
  • Reliable 3D/4D modeling. Geometric and temporal consistency, independent validation, and formal verification of reconstruction and generation.
  • Reliable Blender and 3D tool use. Execution checks, bounded edits, preservation of unaffected geometry, rollback, and recovery.
  • Grounded scene understanding. Hallucination, uncertainty, and abstention; distinguishing observed evidence from inferred or generated content.

Call for Papers

We invite methods, systems, benchmarks, and diagnostic studies on trustworthy agentic 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and modeling.

The workshop is non-archival. Each paper receives three double-blind OpenReview reviews. All accepted papers receive poster slots; three receive oral talks based on quality and topic coverage. Papers are shared through OpenReview and do not appear in IEEE/CVF proceedings.
