Description

Trustworthy agentic 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and modeling, with an emphasis on malicious manipulation, hallucination, reliable tool use, independent checks, and formal verification.

LLM and multimodal agents increasingly reconstruct, generate, and edit 3D/4D models through tools such as Blender. Yet their workflows remain vulnerable to semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, hallucinated geometry, and unsupported claims of successful execution. TrustAgent3D brings together vision, graphics, foundation-model, and security researchers to study trustworthy agentic modeling. We prioritize malicious manipulation and hallucination, alongside reliable tool use and independent verification. Through invited talks, contributed orals, and posters, participants will develop practices connecting scene fidelity, prompt integrity, and recovery across reconstruction, generation, and modeling workflows.

Large language models (LLMs) and multimodal agents are turning 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and editing into iterative tool-using workflows. We use agentic 3D to describe systems that select modeling actions and revise scene representations using feedback. These workflows can interleave code generation, rendering, and visual inspection to construct editable scenes, refine geometry, and model changes over time. Their growing capabilities make trustworthy modeling urgent: a convincing render can conceal incorrect geometry, unintended edits, or unsupported dynamics. Trust therefore requires evidence about both the resulting scene and the sequence of actions used to create it.

Malicious manipulation can redirect these workflows through the information an agent interprets. Semantic prompt injection and jailbreaks can turn scene text, asset metadata, retrieved instructions, or tool responses into instructions that conflict with the user's intent. Traditional pixel- or geometry-level attacks are complementary when they expose related modeling failures. The challenge is to distinguish task evidence from untrusted instructions and prevent compromised feedback from authorizing unwanted edits. Defenses must be evaluated within complete modeling workflows, with explicit attacker access and checks on actual tool actions, while preserving the agent's ability to complete legitimate reconstruction and editing tasks.

Hallucination and ordinary task failure require equally explicit evidence. An agent may invent an object, misread a spatial relation, damage unrelated geometry, or report success despite an incomplete edit. Detecting these failures requires independent geometric checks, scene claims linked to supporting observations, and completion criteria grounded in the actual tool state. Creative generation may intentionally fill unseen regions, but those additions should remain distinguishable from observed geometry. We also welcome formal verification of specified scene properties and tool actions, with explicit assumptions and guarantees. When evidence is insufficient, agents should be able to seek additional observations, express uncertainty, abstain, or recover.

Our central question is: what evidence should an agent require before trusting a 3D scene claim, executing an edit, or reporting success?

  • Q1: How can agents resist malicious scene content and compromised feedback while continuing to complete legitimate 3D tasks?
  • Q2: How can independent geometric and tool-state checks detect failed reconstruction, unintended edits, and unreliable success reports?
  • Q3: How can agents distinguish observed evidence from uncertain inference, and acquire information, abstain, or recover when scene claims are unsupported?

Topics

  • Semantic attacks on 3D agents. Prompt injection, jailbreaks, malicious assets and tool responses, poisoned scene memory, and defenses that preserve legitimate task completion.
  • Reliable 3D/4D reconstruction and generation. Multi-view consistency, scale and coordinate checks, object identity, temporal fidelity, and independent validation or formal verification of scene programs.
  • Reliable Blender and 3D tool use. Execution monitoring, bounded edits, preservation of unaffected geometry, stale-state detection, rollback, and recovery across complete task trajectories.
  • Grounded scene understanding. Hallucination of objects, attributes, spatial relations, and action preconditions; uncertainty, active observation, abstention, and the separation of observed from generated content.

We invite methods, systems, benchmarks, and diagnostic studies on trustworthy agentic 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and modeling.
