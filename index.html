The workshop

What makes an agent's 3D output trustworthy?

LLM and multimodal agents increasingly reconstruct, generate, and edit 3D/4D models through tools such as Blender. Yet their workflows remain vulnerable to semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, hallucinated geometry, and unsupported claims of successful execution. TrustAgent3D brings together vision, graphics, foundation-model, and security researchers to study trustworthy agentic modeling. We prioritize malicious manipulation and hallucination, alongside reliable tool use and independent verification. Through invited talks, contributed papers, posters, and a failure-analysis clinic, participants will develop evaluation practices connecting scene fidelity, instruction integrity, task completion, and recovery across reconstruction, generation, and modeling workflows.

Research context and motivation

LLM and multimodal agents are turning 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and editing into iterative tool-using workflows. VIGA interleaves code generation, rendering, and visual inspection Vision-as-Inverse-Graphics Agent via Interleaved Multimodal Reasoning; LEGO-Anything reconstructs images as editable Blender programs LEGO-Anything; Code4Scene evaluates scene construction and precise editing in Unreal Engine Code4Scene; and 4DCodeBench extends programmatic reconstruction to dynamic scenes 4DCodeBench. We use agentic 3D for systems that select modeling actions and revise scene representations using feedback. Their growing capabilities make trustworthy modeling urgent: a convincing render can conceal incorrect geometry, unintended edits, or unsupported dynamics. Our core scope is agent-driven 3D/4D modeling, with embodied navigation and robot control as adjacent applications.

Malicious manipulation can redirect these workflows through the information an agent interprets. PI3D places instruction-bearing objects in 3D environments Extended to Reality, while CHAI studies command hijacking through deceptive visual signs CHAI. BadNAVer demonstrates jailbreak attacks through navigation instructions BadNAVer. Evidence-carrying multimodal agents instead require verified action preconditions in browser and document workflows Hallucination as Exploit. These studies motivate defenses for scene text, asset metadata, retrieved instructions, and tool responses, but do not establish security for Blender reconstruction. We prioritize semantic prompt injection and jailbreaks; traditional pixel- or geometry-level attacks are complementary when they expose related modeling failures. Evaluations must specify attacker access and distinguish diverted instructions from actual unauthorized edits.

Hallucination and ordinary task failure require equally explicit evidence. BlenderGym benchmarks graphics editing and verification BlenderGym; HEAL examines hallucinations under scene-task inconsistencies HEAL; 3D-GRAND introduces grounded scene-language data and hallucination evaluation 3D-GRAND; and 3D-VCD mitigates unsupported predictions using contrasting scene representations 3D-VCD. Together, they motivate independent geometric checks, evidence-linked scene claims, and reliable completion criteria. Creative generation may intentionally fill unseen regions, but those additions should remain distinguishable from observed geometry. Several recent systems are preprints, and verification remains fallible. Our central question is: what evidence should an agent require before trusting a 3D scene claim, executing an edit, or reporting success?

Three questions for the community

  1. How can agents resist malicious scene content and compromised feedback while continuing to complete legitimate 3D tasks?
  2. How can independent geometric and tool-state checks detect failed reconstruction, unintended edits, and unreliable success reports?
  3. How can agents distinguish observed evidence from uncertain inference, and acquire information, abstain, or recover when scene claims are unsupported?

Research scope

Four connected areas

01

Semantic attacks on 3D agents

Prompt injection, jailbreaks, malicious assets and tool responses, poisoned scene memory, and defenses that preserve legitimate task completion.

02

Reliable 3D/4D reconstruction and generation

Multi-view consistency, scale and coordinate checks, object identity, temporal fidelity, and independent validation of scene programs.

03

Reliable Blender and 3D tool use

Execution monitoring, bounded edits, preservation of unaffected geometry, stale-state detection, rollback, and recovery across complete task trajectories.

04

Grounded scene understanding

Hallucinated objects, attributes, spatial relations, and action preconditions; uncertainty, active observation, abstention, and distinguishing observed from generated content.

Our focus is agent-driven 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and modeling. Semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, and hallucination are central; traditional pixel- and geometry-level attacks are complementary. Embodied navigation and robot control are adjacent applications. Here, 4D means time-varying 3D.

Call for papers

We welcome methods, systems, benchmarks, diagnostic studies, and informative negative results on trustworthy agentic 3D/4D modeling. Claims should be grounded in evidence about the agent, its tools, and the resulting scene.

A shared failure-analysis clinic

Small groups will examine malicious text in a synthetic scene, an edit that damages unrelated geometry, and an unsupported claim about an occluded region. Participants will identify the available evidence, failed checks, and recovery options.

We plan to release a discussion synthesis and reporting checklist within four weeks. The clinic is a discussion exercise; no competition or challenge is proposed.

We will seek sponsorship for student registrations and travel, prioritizing accepted contributors with limited institutional support. No funding is currently secured. Sponsors will have no role in paper decisions.

Impact and ethics

Independent verification can make agentic modeling more useful in graphics production, digital twins, simulation, accessibility, and security engineering. Evaluation should measure scene fidelity and attack resistance together with benign task completion, false rejection, and computational cost.

Research should document data consent, asset licenses, and provenance; protect private environments; and use authorized, isolated attack evaluations. Distinguish observed, inferred, and generated content, disclose evaluator limitations, and sanitize released tool traces. Simulator performance alone does not establish deployment safety.
