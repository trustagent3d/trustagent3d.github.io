Call for papers

Trustworthy Agentic 3D: Addressing Attacks, Failures, and Hallucinations

Submissions are not open. Workshop acceptance and the OpenReview portal are pending. Dates and submission arrangements below are tentative.

Scope

We invite methods, systems, benchmarks, and diagnostic studies on LLM and multimodal agents that reconstruct, generate, or edit 3D/4D models. We prioritize semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, and hallucination, alongside reliable tool use and independent verification. Informative negative results and carefully scoped preliminary studies are welcome.

Topics include malicious scene and tool content, multi-view and temporal consistency, reliable Blender workflows, unintended edits, rollback, and evidence-grounded scene claims. See the four topic areas. Embodied navigation and robot control are adjacent applications; submissions should make their connection to agentic 3D/4D modeling clear.

Submission and publication

  • Format: CVPR 2027 paper style and applicable length restrictions, with a planned maximum of eight content pages excluding references.
  • Review: double-blind review through OpenReview, with three reviews per paper. Submissions should be anonymized.
  • Publication: accepted original research papers are intended for CVPR workshop proceedings, subject to conference eligibility and publication rules.
  • Author requirements: all authors must complete their OpenReview profiles; an author may appear on at most ten submissions to this workshop.
  • Presentation: all accepted papers receive poster slots; six are planned for contributed talks, selected for quality and balanced topic coverage.

The submission portal and final author instructions will be posted after workshop acceptance. There is no competition or challenge.

Review criteria and reporting

Reviewers will assess relevance, technical correctness, strength of evidence, reproducibility, and clarity. Claims should reflect the available evidence, including negative results and residual failures.

  • Agent and environment: specify observations, model configuration, scene representations, tool permissions, and task objectives.
  • Threat model: describe attacker control and distinguish semantic manipulation, ordinary task failure, and unauthorized actions.
  • Grounding and verification: explain what evidence supports scene claims, what the validator checks, and where it can fail.
  • Evaluation: use independent measurements and held-out scenes or views where relevant. Report benign task performance, collateral edits, abstention, and recovery alongside attack resistance.
  • Resources: report compute, repeated verification, and human intervention where relevant. Share code, configurations, and sanitized traces where permitted.

Reviewer capacity and recruitment

External reviewers have not yet been confirmed. We will recruit across semantic attacks on tool-using agents, reconstruction and graphics tools, grounded 3D understanding, and independent verification. Recruitment will include established and qualified early-career researchers across institutions and regions.

For an estimated 25-40 submissions, three reviews per paper and a target load of at most three papers per reviewer require 25-40 active reviewers, plus reserves. Papers on adversarial security will receive review by someone with relevant security expertise.

Authors and reviewers will declare conflicts through OpenReview. Non-conflicted coordinators will handle disagreements; any organizer-authored papers will be handled entirely by independent external reviewers and an external decision maker. Wuyang Chen's CVPR 2027 Workshop Program Chair role will be disclosed, and non-conflicted chairs must assess this workshop proposal without his participation.

Data, ethics, and responsible release

Document consent, asset licenses, and data provenance. Protect private interiors and sensitive locations. Run attacks only in authorized, isolated test environments and avoid releases that expose sensitive targets or unnecessary operational risk.

Distinguish observed, inferred, and generated scene content; disclose uncertainty and evaluator limitations; and sanitize shared tool traces. Do not equate successful simulation with deployment safety. Discuss performance differences across environments and the human oversight needed for consequential actions.
