Call for Papers: CVPR 2027

Trustworthy Agentic 3D: Addressing Attacks, Failures, and Hallucinations

OpenReview submission portal: To be announced after workshop acceptance.

Scope

LLM and multimodal agents increasingly reconstruct, generate, and edit 3D/4D models through tools such as Blender. Yet their workflows remain vulnerable to semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, hallucinated geometry, and unsupported claims of successful execution. TrustAgent3D brings together vision, graphics, foundation-model, and security researchers to study trustworthy agentic modeling. We prioritize malicious manipulation and hallucination, alongside reliable tool use and independent verification. Through invited talks, contributed papers, posters, and a failure-analysis clinic, participants will develop evaluation practices connecting scene fidelity, instruction integrity, task completion, and recovery across reconstruction, generation, and modeling workflows.

See the full topic list. We welcome informative negative results and carefully scoped preliminary studies; novelty alone will not substitute for evidence.

Review process

Each paper receives three double-blind OpenReview reviews. Authors must complete their OpenReview profiles and appear on at most ten submissions. Review criteria include relevance, technical correctness, strength of evidence, reproducibility, and clarity. Reviewers and authors will declare conflicts through OpenReview.

Papers should identify the agent's observations and tool permissions, distinguish security from ordinary task failure, and explain what their validator can and cannot establish. We encourage independent measurements, held-out scenes or views, realistic attacker assumptions, benign task performance, collateral-edit analysis, and reporting of compute and human effort where relevant.

All accepted papers receive poster slots; six receive talks based on quality and topic coverage. No competition is proposed.

Data, reproducibility, and responsible use

Reconstructed environments may reveal private interiors, personal possessions, or sensitive locations. Authors should document consent, asset licenses, data provenance, and measures to reduce exposure of private information. Adversarial studies should use authorized, isolated test environments and avoid publishing sensitive targets or operational details that create unnecessary harm. The clinic will use synthetic scenes and harmless attack objectives. We will ask contributors to distinguish observed, inferred, and generated content, disclose uncertainty and evaluator limitations, and avoid equating simulator performance with deployment safety. Tool traces should be sanitized before release. The discussion will also address unequal performance across environments and the human oversight needed for consequential actions.
