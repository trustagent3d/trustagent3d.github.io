Call for Papers: CVPR 2027

Trustworthy Agentic 3D: Addressing Attacks, Failures, and Hallucinations in Agentic 3D Modeling

OpenReview submission portal: TrustAgent3D at CVPR 2027

Scope

We invite research on trustworthy agentic 3D/4D reconstruction, generation, and modeling. Agentic 3D systems select modeling actions and revise scene representations using observations and tool feedback. Our focus connects scene representations, agentic frameworks, and tool calls, including workflows using Blender and other graphics tools.

Malicious manipulation. We prioritize malicious manipulation and hallucination alongside reliable tool use and independent verification. Semantic prompt injection, jailbreaks, and traditional pixel- or geometry-level attacks are complementary when they expose related modeling failures. Relevant attack surfaces include scene text, asset metadata, retrieved instructions, tool responses, and poisoned scene memory; defenses should preserve legitimate task completion.

Hallucination, task failure, and verification. We welcome independent geometric and tool-state checks, evidence-linked scene claims, reliable completion criteria, and formal verification with explicit assumptions and guarantees. Topics include multi-view and temporal consistency, bounded edits, preservation of unaffected geometry, stale-state detection, rollback, uncertainty, active observation, and abstention. Generated content should remain distinguishable from observed geometry.

The central question is what evidence an agent should require before trusting a 3D scene claim, executing an edit, or reporting success. Evaluation should connect scene fidelity, prompt integrity, and recovery, and assess attack resistance alongside benign task completion, false rejection, and computational cost. Review process

Each paper receives three double-blind OpenReview reviews. Authors must complete their OpenReview profiles and appear on at most ten submissions. Review criteria include relevance, technical correctness, strength of evidence, reproducibility, and clarity. Reviewers and authors will declare conflicts through OpenReview.

Papers should identify the agent's observations and tool permissions, distinguish security from ordinary task failure, and explain what their validator can and cannot establish.

All accepted papers receive poster slots; three receive oral talks based on quality and topic coverage. No competition is proposed.

The current reviewer pool includes 30 confirmed reviewers: 15 in 3D, 10 in AI security, and 5 in general machine learning. The target is 50 by January 2027, with at most three papers per reviewer and three reviews per paper, plus reviewers for emergency assignments.

Further reviewers will be recruited through social-media calls, a Google Form for expertise and availability, and organizer/PC nominations. Expertise and conflicts will be screened to assign complementary reviewers. Reviewers recruited through open channels must have at least one first- or second-author paper at a peer-reviewed ML or scientific venue; organizers will spot-check reviews before decisions.

Each adversarial-security submission will have a reviewer with relevant security expertise. Organizer-authored papers will be handled entirely by independent, non-conflicted external reviewers and an external decision maker.

Data, reproducibility, and responsible use

Reconstructed environments may reveal private interiors, personal possessions, or sensitive locations. Authors should document consent, asset licenses, data provenance, and measures to reduce exposure of private information. Adversarial studies should use authorized, isolated test environments and avoid publishing sensitive targets or operational details that create unnecessary harm. We will ask contributors to distinguish observed, inferred, and generated content, disclose uncertainty and evaluator limitations, and avoid equating simulator performance with deployment safety. Tool traces should be sanitized before release. Workshop discussions will also address unequal performance across environments and the human oversight needed for consequential actions.
